Brick accessibility for special biserial algebras

Every nonsimple brick over a brick-finite special biserial algebra has a brick submodule or quotient of dimension one less, checked in Lean.

Let k be an algebraically closed field and Λ a basic finite-dimensional k-algebra. A brick is a nonzero module whose endomorphism ring is a division ring; over k, a finite-dimensional module M is a brick exactly when End(M) = k.

Theorem A. If Λ is brick-finite and special biserial, every nonsimple brick M has a brick submodule or a brick quotient of dimension dim M − 1.
Corollary B. Every brick over such an algebra is brick accessible, and the brick dimensions of a nonzero such algebra form an interval {1, 2, …, N}.
Corollary 5.6. Both conclusions hold for basic representation-finite biserial algebras. This answers Mousavand’s question on gaps in brick dimensions in the biserial case.

Verification and provenance

The question

Bongartz showed that the lengths of indecomposable modules over a finite-dimensional algebra have no gaps. The analogue for bricks fails: the Kronecker algebra has no brick of dimension 4, and a brick-finite algebra of Aoki, Higashitani, Iyama, Kase and Mizuno has the same gap. Mousavand asked whether brick dimensions have no gaps over representation-finite algebras. The paper proves the stronger accessibility statement for brick-finite special biserial algebras; special biserial is essential, since brick accessibility fails in general.

This repository formalizes the whole paper: Theorem A, Corollary B, Corollary 5.6 and every numbered proposition, lemma, theorem, corollary and remark, including the cited inputs (the Butler–Ringel string and band classification, the Wald–Waschbüsch string quotient and the Skowroński–Waschbüsch theorem). The correspondence record maps each numbered statement to its Lean declarations.
