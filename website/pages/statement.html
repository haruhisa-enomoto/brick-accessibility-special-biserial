The formal theorems

The independent statement uses finite-dimensional right modules, represented in Lean as modules over Λᵐᵒᵖ. The path algebra kQ of a finite quiver is presented by its vertex idempotents and arrows, with paths multiplied in traversal order. An admissible presentation is a surjection π : kQ → Λ whose kernel lies between a power of the arrow ideal and its square.

Λ is special biserial if it has an admissible presentation in which at most two arrows start and at most two end at each vertex, and every arrow has at most one successor and at most one predecessor with nonzero composite. Λ is biserial if the radical of every indecomposable projective right and left module is a sum of at most two uniserial submodules with simple or zero intersection. Brick-finiteness and representation-finiteness count isomorphism classes of finitely generated bricks and indecomposable modules. A brick is brick accessible if it reaches a simple module through brick submodules or quotients, each of dimension one less.

BrickAccessibility.theoremA, corollaryB and corollaryBiserial prove these statements. For Corollary 5.6, isSpecialBiserial_of_biserial constructs the admissible special biserial presentation (Skowroński–Waschbüsch).
