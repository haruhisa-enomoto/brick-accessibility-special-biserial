The proof in three parts

The paper first gives a dimension criterion for smaller bricks, then proves Theorem A for representation-finite gentle algebras through endpoint formulas for string modules, and finally reduces brick-finite special biserial algebras to that case.

Part I: a criterion for smaller bricks

The cut criterion

Over a representation-finite algebra, if dim Hom(S, M) > dim Ext¹(M, S) for a simple S, a nonsimple brick M has a brick quotient of dimension one less; dually for submodules (Proposition 2.1). The formal proof replaces the geometric fibre-dimension step by an explicit polynomial argument.

Cut criterion

Projective covers and inflation

Quotients of a brick by a projective submodule stay bricks (Lemma 2.3), and bricks, submodules and quotients agree over a quotient algebra (Lemma 2.4).

Part II: gentle algebras

Faithful quotients

The faithful quotient of a brick string module is gentle (Proposition 3.8), and the dual of a string module is the string module of the dual word (3.d).

Endpoint formulas

For a gentle string module, s − η and t − θ are given by endpoints and extending arrows (Lemma 4.3), computed with derivations.

Lemma 4.3

Strings and bands

Every indecomposable module over a string algebra is a string module or a band module M(ω, λ, n), and band modules are indecomposable and determined by λ (Theorem 4.5, for every rank).

Theorem 4.5

Positivity

If no endpoint formula has a positive coordinate, extending arrows close up into a band, contradicting finite representation type (Lemma 4.6). This proves the gentle case (Proposition 4.7).

Part III: special biserial algebras

Reduction

Brick-finite gentle algebras are representation-finite (Lemma 5.1), bricks are string modules (Proposition 5.2), and the Wald–Waschbüsch string quotient (Lemma 5.3) separates the nonuniserial projective-injective modules.
